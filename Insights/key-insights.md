Key Insights


Overview



The analysis covers 845 monthly observations from 14 airlines operating arrivals to Boston Logan International Airport between May 2021 and May 2026. The objective was to identify the primary causes of flight delays, evaluate carrier performance, and determine whether delay patterns change over time.



1. Delay rates increase during the summer travel season

The highest delay rates were consistently observed between May and September.

In 2021, the period of increased delays was concentrated between June and August. By 2025, elevated delay rates extended from May through October, indicating a longer period of operational pressure.

This recurring seasonal pattern suggests that airport traffic and operational capacity should be monitored more closely during the summer months.



2. NAS delays dominate during periods of high traffic

The importance of National Air System (NAS) delays increased noticeably during the summer season.

In 2025, nearly 60% of delayed flights during the peak travel period were attributed to NAS-related factors.

Earlier in the analyzed period, carrier-related delays and NAS delays contributed almost equally to the overall number of delayed flights. The results indicate that system-wide operational constraints become increasingly important during periods of high traffic demand.



3. Carrier-related delays have become less significant over time

Carrier-related delays show a gradual downward trend throughout the analyzed period.

Between May and December 2021, carrier-related issues accounted for approximately 40–50% of monthly delays. By 2025, this share had decreased to approximately 15–40%.

Although carrier-related delays remain an important contributor to overall disruption, their relative impact appears to have declined compared to previous years.



4. Delay performance differs substantially between airlines

Delay rates vary considerably across individual carriers.

The airline with the highest historical delay rate no longer operated flights to Boston Logan Airport after 2023.

Among the airlines currently serving the airport, the highest delay rate (approximately 28%) belongs to a carrier responsible for roughly 30,000 arrivals during the analyzed period. Despite being one of the smaller carriers by flight volume, it records one of the poorest punctuality results.

For this carrier:

approximately 40% of delays are carrier-related,
approximately 35.5% result from late arriving aircraft.

Another carrier with a comparable delay rate attributes approximately 47.5% of its delays to National Air System (NAS) factors, indicating that similar punctuality performance may result from different operational challenges.



5. Weather and security contribute relatively little to overall delays

Weather-related and security-related delays represent only a small proportion of total disruptions.

Across the analyzed period:

weather accounted for approximately 0.5% of delays,
security-related delays accounted for approximately 3.7%.

In the most recent year, security-related delays declined further to approximately 1.5%, making them one of the least significant delay categories in the dataset.




Summary


The analysis indicates that seasonal traffic demand and National Air System constraints have the greatest influence on flight punctuality at Boston Logan International Airport. While carrier-related delays remain important, their relative contribution has decreased over the analyzed period. Differences between airlines suggest that operational challenges vary substantially across carriers, highlighting the importance of carrier-specific performance monitoring rather than relying solely on overall delay rates.
