Recommendations


1. Collaborate with carriers experiencing high carrier-related delays

Several airlines exhibit a high proportion of delays classified as carrier-related, indicating that many disruptions originate within airline operations rather than external factors.

Airport management should work directly with these carriers to identify the underlying operational issues, such as aircraft maintenance, crew availability, turnaround processes, or ground handling procedures. Since carrier delays are considered to be within the airline's control, targeted operational improvements may reduce overall delay rates.




2. Investigate the increase in NAS-related delays during peak season

NAS-related delays become the dominant delay category during the summer months, accounting for nearly 60% of delays in the 2025 peak season.

Further investigation should focus on identifying the specific factors contributing to NAS delays, including airport congestion, air traffic control restrictions, runway capacity, and traffic volume. Understanding which operational constraints drive these delays would support more effective planning during periods of increased demand.




3. Prepare for seasonal increases in traffic

The analysis shows a consistent increase in delay rates between May and September, with evidence that this period has gradually extended into October.

Airport operations should consider this seasonal pattern when planning staffing levels, gate allocation, ground handling resources, and runway capacity. Additional monitoring during the summer travel season may help reduce operational bottlenecks.




4. Monitor carrier performance individually

The analysis demonstrates that airlines with similar delay rates often experience delays for different reasons.

Rather than relying solely on overall delay percentages, airport management should monitor the composition of delays for each carrier separately. Tailored operational actions are likely to be more effective than applying the same strategy across all airlines.




5. Expand future analyses with additional operational variables

The current dataset provides valuable information about delay causes but does not include several variables that could explain delay patterns in greater detail.

Future analyses could benefit from incorporating:

day of week,
time of day,
flight destination,
aircraft type,
weather observations,
passenger volume,
runway utilization.

Including these variables would enable deeper analysis and support predictive models for flight delays.
